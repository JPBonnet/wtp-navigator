## Overview

Wtp Navigator serves Dutch SMBs navigating the mandatory pension reform (Wtp). Our three primary personas represent the key decision-makers and influencers in the pension transition purchase process. Understanding their distinct motivations, pain points, and decision criteria is essential for product design, marketing messaging, and sales strategy.

| Persona | Role | Company Size | Primary Motivation |
|---------|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Jan** | Owner-Director | 10–50 employees | Cost savings & simplicity |
| **Sanne** | HR Manager | 50–150 employees | Compliance & guidance |
| **Pieter** | CFO | 100–250 employees | Budget control & data |

## Persona 1: Jan van der Berg — The Owner-Director

### Demographics

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Full name** | Jan van der Berg |
| **Age** | 52 |
| **Role** | Eigenaar-directeur (Owner-Director) |
| **Company** | TechBouw BV — Construction technology, Rotterdam |
| **Company size** | 45 employees |
| **Education** | HBO Bouwkunde (University of Applied Sciences, Construction Engineering) |
| **Decision authority** | Full — signs off on all business purchases |
| **Tech comfort** | Moderate — uses standard business tools, not an early adopter |

### Background & Context

Jan founded TechBouw 15 years ago, growing it from a 3-person operation to a 45-employee company specializing in construction technology solutions. He's a hands-on leader who still visits job sites weekly and personally handles most administrative and financial decisions. His bookkeeper handles payroll, and his accountant handles taxes and annual filings.

Jan set up the company pension through his accountant's recommendation 10 years ago — a basic defined benefit arrangement with a mid-sized pension provider. He pays about €4,500/month in pension contributions and hasn't thought much about it since the initial setup.

Eight months ago, Jan received a letter from his pension provider about the Wtp transition requirements. He mentioned it to his accountant, who said: "Yes, everyone needs to do this, but it's not really my specialty. You might want to talk to a pension consultant." The consultant quoted €12,000 for a full transition plan. Jan put the letter in a drawer and hasn't looked at it since.

### Pain Points

1. **Cost shock:** €12,000 for a pension consultant is hard to justify when his annual profit margin is under €200K. "That's a new piece of equipment I could buy instead."

2. **Complexity overwhelm:** Jan reads about keeping his current age-progressive premium scale (eerbiedigende werking) vs. moving to a flat premium with compensation for older employees, and his eyes glaze over. He doesn't speak pension, and he resents having to learn it for a one-time regulatory exercise.

3. **Trust deficit:** Jan has been burned by advisors before — an insurance broker who oversold disability coverage, a consultant who charged €5,000 for a report that gathered dust. He's skeptical of anyone trying to sell him pension services.

4. **Employee anxiety:** His longest-tenured employee, a 58-year-old project manager, has asked twice about what the pension changes mean for him. Jan doesn't have an answer yet, and the uncertainty is creating unease among his team.

5. **Time poverty:** Between running the business, managing client relationships, and visiting sites, Jan has maybe 2–3 hours per week for administrative tasks. The pension transition feels like a black hole that could consume weeks.

### Buying Triggers

- **Peer influence:** Jan attends a quarterly meetup with other construction company owners. If one of them mentions using a tool that made the pension transition easy, Jan will look into it immediately.
- **Deadline proximity:** Jan operates on the "last responsible moment" principle. At 6 months before the deadline (July 2027), his sense of urgency will spike dramatically.
- **Affordable alternative:** If Jan discovers he can solve this problem for €999 instead of €12,000, the ROI is so obvious he won't need much convincing.
- **Employee pressure:** If more employees start asking about pension changes, Jan will feel the urgency to provide answers and demonstrate he has a plan.

### Decision Criteria (Ranked)

1. **Price** — Must be dramatically cheaper than consultants. Under €1,500 is his comfort zone for a "just do it" decision.
2. **Simplicity** — No jargon. Guide him step by step. If it takes more than an afternoon, he'll procrastinate again.
3. **Speed** — He wants this done, not dragged out over months. Weeks, not months.
4. **Credibility** — It needs to feel legitimate and legally sound. He can't afford to do this wrong.
5. **Independence** — He doesn't want to be locked into a relationship with yet another service provider.

### How Wtp Navigator Serves Jan

- **Free audit** gives him immediate clarity without any commitment or cost
- **€999 price** gets him decision-ready for a fraction of the open-ended consultant quote — an obvious yes (regulated Wft advice, where required, is a clean handoff to a licensed adviser, not something the tool replaces)
- **Plain language** throughout — no pension jargon, just clear instructions
- **Self-service** model respects his independence and time constraints
- **Document generation** gives him something concrete to share with employees and to take into an adviser conversation

### Marketing Messages That Resonate with Jan

- "Your pension consultant quoted €12,000 for an open-ended engagement. Get decision-ready for €999 — then walk in knowing exactly what you want."
- "Get decision-ready on your pension transition in one afternoon, not three months."
- "No pension expertise needed. If you can fill out a tax return, you can use Wtp Navigator."
- "Your employees are asking about the pension changes. Now you can give them answers."

## Persona 2: Sanne de Vries — The HR Manager

### Demographics

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Full name** | Sanne de Vries |
| **Age** | 38 |
| **Role** | HR Manager |
| **Company** | GreenLogistics BV — Sustainable logistics, Utrecht |
| **Company size** | 120 employees |
| **Education** | WO Bedrijfskunde (University, Business Administration) |
| **Decision authority** | Recommends — needs director approval for purchases >€500 |
| **Tech comfort** | High — uses HR software daily, comfortable with SaaS tools |

### Background & Context

Sanne has been the sole HR professional at GreenLogistics for 4 years. Before that, she worked in HR at a larger logistics company where pension was handled by a dedicated compensation & benefits team. At GreenLogistics, she's responsible for everything people-related: recruitment, onboarding, performance management, learning & development, payroll coordination — and now, pension reform compliance.

Her director, Mark, delegated the Wtp transition to her six months ago with a casual "You handle the people stuff, this is a people thing, right?" Sanne quickly realized this was far more complex than a standard HR project. She's attended two webinars, downloaded the government's information package, and started a spreadsheet to track requirements — but she's still not confident she understands what's actually needed.

Sanne has 120 employees across different age groups and salary levels, making the transition more complex than a small company's. She knows that employee communication will be critical — GreenLogistics has had labor disputes in the past, and any perception of unfairness in the pension transition could reignite tensions.

### Pain Points

1. **Personal accountability without expertise:** Sanne is personally responsible for a project that requires specialized knowledge she doesn't have. If something goes wrong, it's on her — but she doesn't have the training to do it right.

2. **Internal justification:** Sanne needs to justify any expenditure to her director. She can't just sign up for a €999 tool — she needs to build a business case and present it. This means the tool needs to look professional and credible enough to survive scrutiny.

3. **Employee communication anxiety:** With 120 employees of varying ages and tenure, the communication challenge is real. Older employees near retirement are especially sensitive. Sanne needs communication templates that are empathetic, accurate, and legally sound.

4. **Compliance uncertainty:** Sanne doesn't know what she doesn't know. She's worried about missing a requirement, filing something incorrectly, or making a decision that seems fine now but creates problems later. She wants a comprehensive checklist that covers everything.

5. **Works council dynamics:** GreenLogistics has an active works council (OR). The OR will need to be consulted on the pension transition, and Sanne needs to manage this process carefully. She's never navigated a major OR consultation before.

6. **Time compression:** Sanne's regular HR responsibilities haven't decreased. The pension transition is on top of everything else. She needs a solution that's efficient and doesn't require her to become a pension expert.

### Buying Triggers

- **Compliance checklist:** If Sanne finds a tool that shows her exactly what's required and what she's missing, she'll feel an immediate sense of relief and motivation to act.
- **Peer validation:** Sanne is active in HR professional networks on LinkedIn. If she sees other HR managers recommending a tool, she'll take notice.
- **Professional presentation:** Sanne needs to present a proposal to her director. A tool that generates professional reports she can attach to her business case will accelerate her decision.
- **OR pressure:** When the works council starts asking about the pension transition timeline, Sanne needs to demonstrate she has a plan and a tool supporting her.

### Decision Criteria (Ranked)

1. **Completeness** — Must cover all compliance requirements, not just the basics. Sanne can't afford gaps.
2. **Professional output** — Reports must be presentation-quality. She needs to share them with her director, the OR, and potentially employees.
3. **Step-by-step guidance** — Not just information, but a process. What to do first, second, third. Clear sequencing.
4. **Support availability** — Sanne will have questions. She needs to know someone competent is available to help when she gets stuck.
5. **Price** — Important but secondary. €999 is easy to justify if the tool delivers on completeness and quality. The real cost barrier is getting director approval, not the price itself.

### How Wtp Navigator Serves Sanne

- **Compliance checklist** provides the comprehensive overview she's been trying to build manually
- **Step-by-step migration planner** gives her a project plan she can follow and share with her director
- **Document generator** produces OR consultation documents and employee communications
- **Professional reports** she can attach to her internal business case
- **Email support** gives her a lifeline when she encounters unfamiliar territory

### Marketing Messages That Resonate with Sanne

- "See exactly where your company stands on Wtp compliance — and what to do next."
- "Generate your transition plan, employee communications, and OR documents in one place."
- "Built for HR professionals who aren't pension specialists."
- "Your director asked you to handle pension reform. Here's how."

## Persona 3: Pieter Bakker — The CFO

### Demographics

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Full name** | Pieter Bakker |
| **Age** | 47 |
| **Role** | CFO |
| **Company** | MediTech Solutions BV — Medical technology, Eindhoven |
| **Company size** | 200 employees |
| **Education** | WO Econometrie (University, Econometrics) + RA (Registeraccountant) |
| **Decision authority** | High — approves all financial decisions, reports to CEO and board |
| **Tech comfort** | High — data-driven, uses financial modeling tools and dashboards |

### Background & Context

Pieter joined MediTech Solutions three years ago after a career in financial management at larger companies. He brought a rigorous, data-driven approach to financial planning that the growing company needed. MediTech is profitable and well-run, but Pieter is cautious with spending — he's seen companies grow too fast and collapse under their own cost structure.

The pension transition is firmly on Pieter's radar because of its financial implications. MediTech currently spends approximately €15,000/month on pension contributions for its 200 employees. The Wtp transition could change this cost structure, and Pieter needs to understand how. He's also concerned about one-time transition costs (consulting fees, compensation arrangements for employees disadvantaged by the change) and wants to minimize surprises.

Pieter has gathered quotes from two pension consultants: one at €18,000 and another at €25,000. Both quoted "plus additional fees for employee-level calculations," making the total cost unpredictable. This lack of cost predictability is Pieter's biggest frustration.

He reports quarterly to MediTech's board, and the pension transition is now a standing agenda item. The board wants clear milestones, costs, and risk assessments — not vague promises from consultants.

### Pain Points

1. **Cost unpredictability:** Open-ended consulting fees with "additional costs TBD" are unacceptable to Pieter. He needs fixed, predictable pricing for everything, including the transition itself and ongoing costs afterward.

2. **Financial impact uncertainty:** How will the new pension arrangement affect MediTech's monthly costs? What are the one-time transition costs? What compensation might be needed for employees who are disadvantaged? Pieter needs models, not guesses.

3. **Board reporting burden:** Every quarter, Pieter needs to present pension transition progress to the board. He needs clear, data-rich reports that show status, costs, risks, and timeline — not narrative summaries.

4. **ROI justification:** As CFO, Pieter evaluates every expenditure on ROI. For pension transition tools, the ROI calculation is: total cost of our approach vs. total cost of alternatives, including hidden costs, delays, and risk.

5. **Multi-scenario planning:** Pieter wants to model different scenarios — what if they keep the current age-progressive premium scale (eerbiedigende werking) vs. move to a flat premium with compensation? What if they invest more in compensation vs. less? He needs comparative analysis, not a single recommendation. (Note: "invaren" — collective conversion of accrued rights — is a pension-*fund* mechanism and does not apply to MediTech's insured scheme; the defining decision is eerbiedigende werking vs. flat premium with compensation.)

6. **Integration with financial systems:** Pieter wants pension transition data to flow into his financial planning. Exporting data to Excel is a minimum requirement. Ideally, the tool produces outputs compatible with his reporting framework.

### Buying Triggers

- **Fixed pricing:** When Pieter discovers a €999 fixed-price alternative to €18,000–€25,000+ variable-price consulting, the financial case is immediately compelling.
- **Data quality demonstration:** If the free audit produces a detailed, accurate analysis that matches what consultants would deliver, Pieter will be convinced of the tool's value.
- **Board pressure:** When the board asks for a specific timeline and cost estimate for the pension transition, Pieter needs a tool that can produce this information quickly and credibly.
- **Peer CFO recommendation:** Pieter is part of a CFO network. A recommendation from a peer carries significant weight.

### Decision Criteria (Ranked)

1. **Total cost of ownership** — Not just the tool cost, but the total cost of the pension transition. Does using this tool reduce overall transition costs compared to alternatives?
2. **Data accuracy and depth** — Financial projections must be reliable. Pieter will spot-check numbers against his own calculations. If the tool's numbers don't add up, he'll reject it immediately.
3. **Predictability** — Fixed pricing, clear scope, defined timeline. No open-ended commitments or variable fees.
4. **Reporting capability** — Board-ready reports, exportable data, clear visualizations of financial impact.
5. **Efficiency** — Minimize the time investment from Pieter and his team. The tool should reduce work, not create it.

### How Wtp Navigator Serves Pieter

- **€999 fixed price** eliminates cost unpredictability — the total is known before purchase
- **Financial impact modeling** shows exactly how different pension choices affect the P&L
- **Scenario comparison** allows Pieter to evaluate eerbiedigende werking vs. a flat premium with compensation using hard numbers
- **Board-ready reports** with professional formatting and clear data visualizations
- **Export capabilities** for integration with existing financial planning tools
- **Cost comparison generator** that clearly demonstrates savings vs. consultant approach

### Marketing Messages That Resonate with Pieter

- "One price. No surprises. No 'additional fees TBD.' Just €999 for everything."
- "See exactly how the pension transition affects your P&L — before you commit."
- "Generate board-ready pension transition reports in minutes, not weeks."
- "Your consultants quoted €18,000 with variable extras. Get decision-ready for a fixed €999 — then hand off cleanly to your Wft adviser for the regulated advice."
